DPIA & LIA Assessments

Statutory Section 28 & High-Risk Diagnostic

Anticipate, quantify, and neutralize high-risk data processing hazards

Under Section 28 of the Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) 2023, performing a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) is a mandatory statutory obligation prior to initiating processing likely to result in high risk to the rights and freedoms of individuals.

Whether deploying AI underwriting algorithms, biometric KYC onboarding, enterprise CCTV surveillance, or digital health monitoring, our accredited DPCO practice executes exhaustive DPIAs and 3-part Legitimate Interests Assessments (LIAs) that ensure regulatory approval and operational resilience.

  • Statutory Section 28 high-risk diagnostic screening and threat modeling
  • Rigorous 5x5 likelihood-impact matrix with turnkey engineering mitigations
  • Representation during NDPC Section 31 prior regulatory consultations

Frequently Asked Questions

Under NDPA Section 28, a DPIA is mandatory before launching: systematic profiling or automated decision-making (e.g. credit scoring), large-scale processing of sensitive data (biometrics, health, religious beliefs), public monitoring (CCTV networks), or introducing disruptive innovative technologies.
The LIA consists of: 1) The Purpose Test (is there a genuine commercial or public benefit?), 2) The Necessity Test (is processing personal data essential and proportionate?), and 3) The Balancing Test (do the fundamental rights and expectations of the individual override the controller's interest?).
Under NDPA Section 31, if a DPIA indicates that processing would result in high residual risk that cannot be mitigated by reasonable technical controls, the data controller must consult the NDPC prior to processing. Amstel Consulting prepares the statutory prior consultation dossier.
